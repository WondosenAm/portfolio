Who to call: bank telemarketing analysis

Data analysis and machine learning · Python, pandas, scikit-learn, SciPy, statsmodels, SHAP

The question
A Portuguese bank phoned customers to sell term deposits, and only 11.3% of calls ended in a subscription. Which customers are worth calling?
Data
41,188 calls with customer, campaign and economic details, from a public dataset (Moro, Cortez and Rita, 2014).
What I did
Cleaned the data without throwing customers away: kept “unknown” answers as their own category, removed 12 duplicates, and turned the “999 = never contacted” code into a yes/no flag. Explored segments with effect sizes, VIF, PCA and Bayesian probability, and grouped customers with K-Means. Left call duration out of every model, because it’s only known after the call ends.
Result
Customers whose previous campaign succeeded subscribed at about 65%, 5.8 times the average. Three models reached a ROC-AUC of about 0.80. Calling the top 20% of customers by score would reach 66% of subscribers, compared with 20% at random.

Malaysian Sign Language recognition

Computer vision and deep learning · Group project, Universiti Malaya · Python, PyTorch, MediaPipe, OpenCV, Flask

The question
Can a webcam recognize Malaysian Sign Language gestures as they happen?
Data
About 3,900 recorded sequences of 51 gestures. MediaPipe turns each frame into 258 body and hand landmark values, and the model reads 30 frames at a time.
What we did
Tried a simple baseline and a BiLSTM, then built a model with 1D convolution layers and a Transformer encoder in PyTorch. Wrapped it in a Flask app for live camera and video uploads that shows a gesture only when the model is at least 60% confident.
Result
94.9% accuracy on 584 held-out sequences. The right gesture was among the top three predictions 99% of the time.

English–Wolayita machine translation

Natural language processing · Python, TensorFlow, Keras, NLTK

The question
How much does attention help a translation model trained on little data?
Data
About 27,000 English sentences paired with Wolayita translations. Wolayita is a language spoken in southern Ethiopia.
What I did
Built an LSTM encoder–decoder and trained it twice: once on its own, and once with a Luong attention layer that lets the decoder look back at each source word.
Result
The BLEU score on held-out sentences rose from 2.35 to 4.59 with attention. Both scores are low: short phrases translate better than long sentences, which often come out incomplete.

Chronic kidney disease stage classification

Machine learning · Python, pandas, scikit-learn, CatBoost, Gradio

The question
Can routine test results place a patient in one of five stages of chronic kidney disease?
Data
About 9,000 patient records with 25 fields, such as age, blood pressure, blood and urine tests, hypertension and diabetes.
What I did
Cleaned the records, filled missing values and removed outliers with Tukey’s rule. Labeled each record by its estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), balanced the classes with SMOTE, and picked 10 features with an ANOVA F-test.
Result
Compared seven classifiers, from logistic regression to CatBoost, and put the random forest behind a small Gradio form where you enter a patient’s values and get a stage.

Survey campaign analysis

Data analysis · Python, pandas, matplotlib, seaborn

The question
Which age groups, genders and time frames had the best completion rates in an online survey campaign in the United States?
Data
1,064 campaign records, 935 after removing incomplete rows.
What I did
Dropped unused tracking fields, calculated age from date of birth, grouped ages into bands, and broke responses down by age group, gender, state, device and browser.
Result
The median respondent was 49 and 55% were women. Every response came from a phone or tablet browser.
